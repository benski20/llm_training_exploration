Private AI without a box on site · design note

Masked cloud inference with one-time pads

The customer's text never leaves their building in readable form, the heavy model runs on our GPUs, and the customer's laptop does almost no work while it waits for an answer.

How it works

Orange is secret and stays on site. The patterned boxes are what travels over the internet. Each mask is used once and then thrown away.

In plain terms

Think of a calculator you don't trust. You want it to compute 3 × 7 without learning the 3. So you add a secret number you picked last night, say 40, and send 43. The calculator returns 43 × 7 = 301. You already worked out 40 × 7 = 280 last night, so you subtract and get 21. The calculator only ever saw 43, and since 40 was random, 43 tells it nothing about your 3.

An AI model is mostly huge multiplications like that. The model is public, so the customer can prepare the "40 × 7" part ahead of time for every step. During the actual question the laptop only adds and subtracts, and our GPUs do the expensive multiplying on numbers that look like static.

  • Overnight · customer

    Make masks

    A spare computer picks random numbers and pre-computes how the model would transform them. Results go on a USB drive or local disk.

  • Question time · laptop

    Hide the data

    Add a fresh random mask to each step's numbers. What leaves the building is indistinguishable from noise.

  • Question time · our GPU

    Do the heavy maths

    Multiply the noise by the public model. Same cost as normal AI, roughly.

  • Question time · laptop

    Unhide the result

    Subtract the pre-computed answer for the mask. The real result appears. Repeat for each layer.

A cheaper 32-bit version cuts these by about a third, but needs 8-bit activations. In our quality check that changed 7% of the model's top predictions, against 2.7% for 12-bit and above. A 40-bit middle setting with 12-bit activations keeps quality and saves about a sixth.

Trade-offs to know

  • Masks get used up. Every word read or written uses one set (8 MB for an 8B model). They must be refilled, like ink.
  • Data volume. About 13 MB crosses the internet per word for an 8B model. A 2,000-word document is about 27 GB: minutes on a fast office line.
  • Making masks is real work. It is the same maths as running the model, six times over. A cheap GPU makes 8B masks for well under a dollar per million words; ordinary CPUs manage a few words per second.
  • Many short trips. Each word needs a round trip per layer section (about 128 for an 8B model). Fine for documents; for chat, our servers need to be close to the customer.
  • The mask drive is a key. Someone who steals it and our server logs could read the data. Store it like a security key.
  • Privacy holds even if we cheat. A dishonest server could return wrong numbers, which spot checks catch, but it can never read anything.

Making it easier for the customer

Install an app, not a box

A small background agent, like a VPN client, makes masks during idle hours on machines the customer already owns, and exposes a normal chat window and API. No USB handling for most users.

Masks from the customer's own IT

One existing server makes masks for the whole organisation overnight and hands them out on the internal network. Laptops do nothing but add and subtract.

Store a seed, not the mask

The random mask regenerates from a 32-byte seed, so only the pre-computed answers are stored. This halves storage.

Spend masks only on secrets

Public text (system prompts, policy documents, templates) goes through without masks or is cached once. Only the private parts consume the mask budget.

Process documents in batches

All words of a document go through each layer together, so round trips depend on model depth rather than document length. Best fit: summarising, extracting and classifying files.

Independent mask provider

The most practical setup. A separate company, on separate GPUs, makes masks for about the cost of our own serving and streams them to the customer. Safe as long as the two companies never share data.

Use 8-bit activations

Standard W8A8 quantization lets the maths run in 32-bit numbers instead of 48-bit. That cuts masks, data volume and mask making by about a third.

Tested on Kaggle with Qwen2.5-0.5B end to end, and timed at Llama-3-8B and 70B layer sizes. Integer arithmetic modulo 248, 8-bit weights, 20-bit activations.
